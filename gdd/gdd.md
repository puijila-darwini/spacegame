# Working Design Document — Slow Galactic Trading Game (v0 skeleton)

Source: ChatGPT thread "Slow Trading Game Concept" (shared 2026-09-10,
pulled in full) + owner voice-rumble + interface/orbit/naming deltas.
Pin the economic and temporal model; do NOT yet spec hundreds of
commodities/ships/factions.

## 1. Premise

Persistent, asynchronous space trading. You begin as a small independent
trader with one ship and limited capital; you end running a distributed
commercial organisation. You are a **capital allocator and merchant**,
not a pilot. Core fantasy: *put capital somewhere in the galaxy, wait,
find out what happened.* Minutes of attention = days/weeks of sim time.

## 2. Fundamental laws

1. **Distance costs time.** Ships move continuously; travel in days/weeks.
2. **dv costs money.** Moving mass between gravity wells is expensive and
   constrains what is worth trading.
3. **Information is imperfect and delayed.** Distant market state arrives
   via slow public channels and faster/better personal contacts.
4. **Markets are illiquid.** No universal price; only inventories, buyers,
   sellers, contracts, relationships.

## 3. Player role

Buy/sell cargo, charter/own ships, pick destinations and trajectories,
negotiate, take contracts, finance voyages, employ captains and agents,
open offices/warehouses, buy industrial assets, smuggle, build
infrastructure, cultivate firms/governments. No manual piloting — a ship
may burn twelve days while you do something else.

## 4. Galaxy (graph first)

Nodes: planets, moons, habitats, stations, mines, industrial facilities,
jump infrastructure. Edges: viable trajectories with
`time / dv / fuel-energy / risk / nav + political constraints`.
Route shape: `planet → orbit → station → jump point → transit →
jump point → station`. Player always sees asset positions + ETAs.

## 5. Transportation (dv-conscious)

Ships have cargo/dry mass, efficiency, dv budget, accel, fuel/energy,
operating + life-support costs, crew. No fast travel: low-energy runs
take weeks, high-energy runs take days at steep cost. Core question:
*is this cargo's destination value worth the mass it costs to move?*
Ship archetypes: bulk hauler (huge, slow, efficient, inflexible) vs
courier (tiny, huge dv, expensive, high-value goods). Chains
(`Earth → depot → depot → Mars`) and infrastructure (depots, propellant,
elevators, mass drivers, yards, warehouses) can make routes viable —
position in the network beats owning a mine. Within-system = dv economy;
between-system = jump-capacity + energy + time.

## 6. Economy (local, fragmented)

Many local economies, not one market. Each location: population,
production/consumption, inventories, industries, firms, government,
infrastructure, local prices, shortage/surplus, labour, capital. Trade
exists because of persistent differences in resources, industry, labour,
tech, biology, capital, infrastructure, politics.

## 7. Commodities (by function, not terrestrial names)

- **Bulk materials** (ore, metals, water, volatiles, carbon, silicates,
  feedstock): low value/kg, dv-and-well dominated. Key flow often *down*
  the well to orbital industry. Tiny margins, enormous volumes.
- **People** (migration, skilled/specialists, colonists, crews, contracts,
  refugees, prisoners, dependants): tiny mass, huge value spread, but
  life-support/legal/medical/reputation constraints. Human capital as
  scarce local resource drives migration.
- **Biologics** (food, seed, genetics, pharma, cultures, engineered
  organisms, specimens): highest value density; perishable, restricted,
  ecological consequences. A shipment can be a *capability* (seed stock
  founds an industry).
- **Spacefaring inputs** (propellant, reactor materials, electronics,
  sensors, machinery, parts, structures, life-support, ship/station gear):
  the heart — demand generated by the fleet itself
  (`trade → ships → input demand → industry → more trade`).
- **Contraband is a legal status, not a category.** Same object legitimate
  here, prohibited there. Premium from value-density + prohibition +
  imperfect enforcement + info asymmetry. Needs routes, concealment, crew,
  corrupt officials, safe ports, false manifests — the contact network.

## 8. Markets (order books, not tickers)

Never `Titanium: 42cr`. Instead `Seller A 80t@41 / B 250t@44…`,
`Buyer A wants 100t ≤52…`, of which you know a subset. Public price is an
observation (`last: 43cr, 60t, 3d ago`). Big buys exhaust supply and move
the market. Finding counterparties is gameplay.

## 9. Information (three tiers)

Public (slow, wide, reliable-ish) → contacts (local knowledge:
"refinery lost two lines") → agents (act for you: "negotiate the
contract"). Progression: knowing prices → knowing happenings → knowing
who knows → having someone who acts.

## 10. Contacts

Real NPCs: occupation, location, expertise, reliability, reputation,
wealth, connections, politics, interests, willingness. Built through
transactions: `shortage on Mars` → `my yard contact needs 4000t of X in
40d` → `my agent negotiates procurement`. The network is an asset.

## 11. Events (drive prices)

Originate locally, propagate: crop failure, accident, discovery,
epidemic, war, blockade, crisis, station loss, reactor failure,
breakthrough, regulation, embargo, migration, bankruptcy. Chains like
`mine accident → metal price → yard stocks → shipbuilding → parts demand
→ neighbours respond → traders redirect`. Player sees fragments.

## 12. Contracts

Second trade mode beyond speculation: `800t titanium in 60d ≤1200/t`,
`200 specialists before construction season`. Turn uncertain demand into
something financeable; make relationships pay.

## 13. Finance (later layer)

Trade credit, loans, cargo/ship financing, insurance, forwards,
warehouse receipts, ownership. Path:
`merchant → trading company → logistics co → financial institution`
without becoming an accounting sim.

## 14. Infrastructure (changes economics, not +10%)

Warehouses, fuel stations, depots, yards, mines, plants, habitats,
comms, offices, customs. A propellant depot can permanently reroute
regional trade.

## 15. Time and offline

Not idle (`wait → coins → upgrade`). Loop:
`observe → decide → commit → wait → consequences`. Morning check finds
arrivals, acceptances, mail, collapses, wars, captain problems.

## 16. Progression (agency, not numbers)

One ship + relationships → ships + captains/agents/contracts/warehouses
→ offices + industry + finance + infrastructure → regional-economy
shaping institution. Trader → merchant → company → institution.

## 17. Central loop

Something happened somewhere → who knows? → what changes? → where do
consequences land? → can I position capital/cargo first? → can I move it
profitably? → who buys on arrival? → what happened en route?

## 18. Prototype scope

6–10 locations, 3–5 economies, 5–10 commodities/ships, ~20 NPCs, events,
trajectory sim, markets, contacts, contracts, offline progress. Fun test:
*"I decided 3 days ago; I open now and learn if I was right."*

## 19. Interface (owner delta 2026-09-10)

KSP-meets-trader. 2D ecliptic system view (planets on orbits, ships on
Hohmann arcs with ETA labels, click-to-plot, window countdowns) + galaxy
jump graph (`~Xw / jump-cost` edges) + side panels (ship; port with only
*known* counterparties/prices; commit preview `mass×dv×time×risk vs
value`) + time controls (pause, 1d/7d) + event/factor ledger. Interstellar
jump stays subtle (fade/stretch); the payoff is the arrival report.
V0 = static mockups; sim exposes `bodies(t)`, `ships(t, traj, eta)` so
any renderer (web-canvas single-file or pygame) just draws.

## 20. Orbits and windows (owner delta 2026-09-10)

Keplerian-lite, NOT fully fake: circular coplanar orbits, real periods
(`n = sqrt(mu/a^3)`, `angle = angle0 + n·t`), analytic Hohmann arcs
(`t = π√((a1+a2)³/8μ)`), phase-gated departures. Waiting is a capital
decision; off-window fast transfers allowed at steep premium. Edge
contract: `next_window(t), wait, hohmann_dv/time, fast_dv(t)`.

## 21. Names (owner delta 2026-09-10)

Planets/worlds use common English names only, mirrored from
`pthmud/world/names.py` at v0: Moon, Tide, Veluvy, Nellus, Sun, Arax,
Fulmaior. No factions, no Greek/Latin/OE variants in v0. Fixed
stars/systems are Bayer + zodiac (`Alpha Phocae`, `Beta Vermis`, …; 12
signs = 12 constellations). `stars.csv`/Latin constellations unused.

## 22. Scales are real, and the clock is not attached to them (owner delta 2026-09-26)

Planetocentric `a` is a real fraction of the heliocentric chart: Moon 0.26% of
Tern's orbit (Earth/Moon 0.257%), Cteta 0.22% of Fulmaior's (Callisto 0.24%).
The clock belongs to the parent's `mu`. Because `n = sqrt(mu/a³)`, rescaling a
moon's radius by `k` and its parent's `mu` by `k³` leaves the period untouched —
so the radii can be honest without touching MUD-literal periods. They WERE
20–29% once, to make zoomed system views look right; that put a visible ring
around every world in the heliocentric view.

The 400:1 span between a planetary orbit and a moon orbit is handled by the
renderer, not by lying about the data:

- **System view fits the outermost ring** — absolute scale is invisible there,
  only ratios matter. `PLANET_DISC_DIV = 60` is the real Earth/Moon figure, and
  it is what leaves a synchronous shell at 5.6 disc-radii clear of the planet.
  `MAX_ZOOM = 6000` to reach a real moon system.
- **Heliocentric view suppresses sub-5px rings.** A moon system IS sub-pixel
  from across a system; drawing it anyway stacked a ring on the planet.
- **The star scales with the innermost planetary orbit** so its glow cannot
  eclipse the inner system (it was a fixed 26px glow over a 22px inner system).

Rendered ordering is an ordering, not a vibe: innermost moon at ~42–60 disc
radii, synchronous shell at ~9% of the inner moon (Earth 9.3%), station inside
that and faster than the ground. Rings outside the moons make a system read
inside-out. Planetary radii are Kepler-consistent with MUD-literal periods, so
their radial gaps (1.31×, 2.23×, 5.24×, 1.80×) are forced by `T ∝ a^1.5`.

**Open, and deliberately not decided here:** a linear radial axis over a 27×
span (0.342 → 9.43) puts the inner three planets inside ~22px of the star
while the outer two dominate. The honest fix is a compressed radial projection,
but it must warp the Hohmann arcs identically or ships visibly detach from their
planets mid-flight. That is a design decision, not a tweak.

## 23. Space elevators are synchronous (owner delta 2026-09-26)

A tether's counterweight must co-rotate with the ground, so **an elevator
terminal's orbital period is its host's sidereal rotation** (`state.ROTATION`,
0.9–1.8d; `state.orbit_geometry` takes the terminal period straight from it).
That co-rotation is the whole reason a tether is stable and why the 0.006dv
ride undercuts a rocket climb — the terminal is never moving relative to the
ground it is tied to. Inventing a period per terminal kind (it used to be a flat
1.8d for every terminal) severs the link to the planet the tether hangs from.

Stations are ordinary low orbit: inside the synchronous shell at 0.55 of its
radius, and faster than the rotation (0.16×). The synchronous radius sits at
~9% of the inner moon, matching Earth's 9.3%.

## 24. The Fulmaior Gate (owner delta 2026-09-26)

First inter-system anchor, beyond Fulmaior (a=15.0 vs 9.43). Drawn as two
counter-rotating rings, not a disc: it is an aperture, not a world.

Lifecycle is time-driven so the far side is something the player watches
approach rather than a surprise: `sealed` → `stabilising` (120d out) →
`open` at t=400d, ~1.1 Tern years.

**Transit is refused, not faked.** `orbits` blocks the gate in both
`next_window` and `fast_option` because no delta-v budget or wait time
buys a crossing that doesn't exist. The far side is fixture data
(`state.build_alpha_phocae()`), not a loaded system. This is deliberate
scope: the anchor and the promise are real, the crossing is the next
build. What earns inter-system travel is a freight market with a
17-year Sol clock on the far side — not a menu item.

- Renderer choice (web single-file vs pygame) deferred past mockups.
